Cyrus-H04: Biomechanical Principles, Analytical Reflex Kinematics, and Residual Reinforcement Learning for Omnidirectional Push Recovery in Full-Scale Bipedal Humanoids

Prajval Darur, Cyrus Autonomous Robotics Research Group
Project Cyrus Humanoid Initiative • November 2026 • Technical Report CYRUS-TR-2026-H04-REV3
Abstract
Dynamic balance and push recovery in full-scale bipedal humanoids represent one of the most challenging frontiers in modern robotics due to non-linear multibody dynamics, strict friction cone constraints, unilateral ground contacts, and the underactuated nature of floating-base systems. This paper presents the complete architectural, mathematical, and control formulation of Cyrus-H04, an open-source, production-grade 12-DoF lower-body and 8-DoF upper-body humanoid robot capable of surviving violent omnidirectional impulse disturbances up to 0.80 m/s and continuous stochastic turbulence.

We identify and resolve the three fatal failure modes of conventional tabula-rasa reinforcement learning (RL): the "Statue vs. Sprinter" gradient conflict, the inverse kinematics exploration trap, and temporal rollout data dilution. To overcome these limitations, Cyrus-H04 integrates a Hierarchical 3-Tier Balance System: (1) An Ankle Strategy utilizing Center of Pressure (CoP) modulation within the support polygon (< 0.25 m/s); (2) A Whole-Body Angular Momentum Strategy generating dynamic counter-moments (\(\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{CoM}}\)) via coordinated arm swings and lumbar pitch to cancel up to 40% of shove kinetic energy without lifting a foot (0.25–0.60 m/s); and (3) An Analytical Capture Point Stepping Reflex coupled with a Residual Reinforcement Learning Layer for violent shocks (> 0.60 m/s).

The analytical reflex provides 80% of the macroscopic trajectory via closed-form 3D leg inverse kinematics (IK) and Divergent Component of Motion (DCM) equations, while an Asymmetric Actor-Critic neural network trained across 1,024 parallel environments in JAX/MJX learns the remaining 20% residual compliance, ground contact damping, and foot friction adaptation. We demonstrate zero-scuffing dynamic clearance (\(z_{\text{clear}} = 0.090\,\text{m}\)) and stance-ankle flat-foot compensation (\(\Delta q = -0.060\,\text{rad}\)), validated through full 5.0-second (250-step at 50 Hz) native MuJoCo physics audits across all 360° directions.

2. Fundamental Biomechanics: The 3 Balance Strategies

In terrestrial biomechanics, balance maintenance is categorized into three distinct strategies based on perturbation magnitude and the location of the Divergent Component of Motion relative to the Base of Support:

  • Strategy 1: Ankle Strategy (\(\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{CoM}}\| < 0.25\,\text{m/s}\)): Modulates the Center of Pressure (CoP) under the foot soles without lifting the feet: $$\mathbf{p}_{\text{CoP}} = \frac{\mathbf{n} \times \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ankle}}}{F_z}$$
  • Strategy 2: Whole-Body Momentum & Hip Strategy (\(0.25\,\text{m/s} \le \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{CoM}}\| \le 0.60\,\text{m/s}\)): Actuates the upper torso and dual 4-DoF arms to generate counter-moments: $$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{restoring}} = \frac{d\mathbf{H}}{dt} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left( \mathbf{I}_i \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i + m_i (\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_{\text{CoM}}) \times \ddot{\mathbf{r}}_i \right)$$ Canceling up to 40% of shove kinetic energy without foot lift.
  • Strategy 3: Stepping Recovery Reflex (\(\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{CoM}}\| > 0.60\,\text{m/s}\)): The Capture Point exits the foot polygon; an explosive 50 Hz swing is triggered to reposition the base of support.

3. Mathematical Derivations & Analytical Stepping Kinematics

3.1 Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM) & Orbital Frequency

Constraining the Center of Mass to a constant height \(z_c = 0.783\,\text{m}\) yields:

$$\ddot{\mathbf{r}}_{\text{CoM}} = \frac{g}{z_c} (\mathbf{r}_{\text{CoM}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{CoP}})$$ $$\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{g}{z_c}} = \sqrt{\frac{9.80665\,\text{m/s}^2}{0.783\,\text{m}}} \approx 3.5383\,\text{rad/s}$$

3.2 Divergent Component of Motion (Capture Point)

$$\boldsymbol{\xi} = \mathbf{r}_{\text{CoM}} + \frac{\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{\text{CoM}}}{\omega_0}, \quad \dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}} = \omega_0 (\boldsymbol{\xi} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{CoP}})$$

3.3 Dynamic Step Trigger & Target Foot Placement

$$\mathbf{x}_{\text{land}} = \boldsymbol{\xi} + k_{\text{margin}} \dot{\mathbf{r}}_{\text{CoM}}, \quad k_{\text{margin}} = 0.050\,\text{s}$$
  • Dynamic Flight Clearance: \(z_{\text{clear}} = 0.090\,\text{m}\) (+5 cm lift) eliminates foot scuffing.
  • Stance Flat-Foot Ankle Compensation: \(\Delta q_{\text{ankle\_pitch}} = -0.060\,\text{rad}\) cancels stance knee dip (+0.12 rad), maintaining a 100% flat sole.
  • Lateral Overturning Moment Cancellation: Lateral stance hip roll shift \(\Delta q = +0.060\,\text{rad}\) eliminates the \(66.2\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}\) lateral overturning torque during single support.

4. The Residual Reinforcement Learning Architecture

Rather than learning motor angles from scratch (tabula rasa), Cyrus couples the analytical kinematic reflex with a residual policy:

$$\mathbf{u}_{\text{target}} = \text{clip}\left( \mathbf{q}_{\text{reflex}}(\mathbf{s}) + \mathbf{a}_{\text{residual}} \odot \mathbf{v}_{\text{scale}}, \mathbf{u}_{\text{min}}, \mathbf{u}_{\text{max}} \right)$$
  • 80% Control Effort (\(\mathbf{q}_{\text{reflex}}\)): Analytical Capture Point IK places the foot where physics dictates.
  • 20% Residual Compliance (\(\mathbf{a}_{\text{residual}}\)): Neural network learns ground friction adaptation and impact damping.
  • State-Conditioned Reward Gating: When \(\text{needs\_step} = \text{True}\), pose and symmetry penalties are zeroed (0.0), allowing compliant crouching and single-leg swing.

5. Hardcore Perturbation Swarm Engine

  • Settling Grace Period: Cut to 12 steps (0.24 s)—the exact physical duration for sole ground contact.
  • Early Shock Cascades: Shocks hit at step 15 (\(t = 0.30\,\text{s}\)), step 75, step 140, step 220, and step 300.
  • Continuous Stochastic Background Turbulence: Persistent horizontal Gaussian force chatter \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{turb}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\) on the torso ensures the robot never relaxes into a rigid "statue mode".

7. Conclusion

Cyrus-H04 demonstrates that high-performance bipedal humanoid push recovery is maximized by marrying analytical Divergent Component of Motion physics with compliant residual reinforcement learning. The addition of dynamic ground clearance, flat-foot ankle compensation, and upper-body momentum decoupling provides a complete, robust, and mathematically grounded foundation for real-world humanoid deployment.
